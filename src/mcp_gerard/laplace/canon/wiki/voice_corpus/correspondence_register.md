**Two distinct correspondence sub-registers confirmed.**
CORR-01 through CORR-03 are personal/confessional letters; CORR-04 through CORR-15 are peer reviews. The peer reviews form a coherent sub-register. The personal letters are closer to the personal essay register. These should not be conflated. The peer review sub-register is the one with direct manuscript-transfer value.

**Peer review opening structure (CORR-04 through CORR-15).**
All peer reviews follow the same opening logic: acknowledge merit or signal collaborative engagement, then state the precise problem, then itemise. The merit acknowledgment is not flattery; it is the ground from which all subsequent critique operates. Remove the merit acknowledgment and the critique loses its authority. In manuscript introductions, the equivalent move is: state the existing result or the established framework before naming what is missing.

**Permission-granting and scope acknowledgment (CORR-05, CORR-06, CORR-11, CORR-13).**
The peer review voice consistently grants permission to the authors: "The authors may consider their response to any and all of these points optional." "This is a suggestion the authors are free to ignore." "I appreciate this may be beyond the scope of the current paper." This is not weakness; it is a technique for separating required changes from desirable ones. In manuscript writing, the equivalent is explicit scope-setting: state what the present work does and does not claim.

**Root-cause diagnosis over symptom-listing (CORR-07, CORR-09, CORR-14).**
The most effective peer reviews diagnose a single root problem from which all others follow, rather than listing independent errors. "The manuscript tends to present lengthy formulae in the main text while also relegating entire derivations to the appendices. This arrangement dilutes the effectiveness of both." In manuscript prose, the equivalent is: identify the structural object whose absence creates the gap, rather than listing the gaps independently.

**Questions as collaborative tools (CORR-05, CORR-06, CORR-10, CORR-12, CORR-15).**
The peer review voice uses questions to open directions rather than to expose failures: "Have the authors considered a Floquet drive after deparametrisation?" "Is there any way to interrogate whether there exist other frames?" The question is genuine; it is not a rhetorical device for implying the answer is no. In manuscript prose, the equivalent is: end a section by naming what the result opens, not only what it closes.

**Limit-as-invitation (CORR-12, CORR-13, CORR-15).**
The voice consistently converts a missing piece into an intellectual invitation rather than a deficiency: "I should note I don't expect an answer to this question. Nevertheless, I do think it is perhaps an issue worth thinking more deeply on." In manuscript prose, the equivalent is: when naming a limitation, name also the question the limitation opens.

**Controlled firmness in the harshest mode (CORR-14).**
When the peer review voice delivers a rejection, it achieves maximum force through parallelism and pre-emptive closure: "What is true in this paper is not novel, and what is novel is untrue." "Any attempt to dispute this must rely on a faulty assumption." The firmness is structural, not emotional; it comes from logical necessity, not from irritation. In manuscript prose, the equivalent is: when a prior claim is incorrect, show explicitly that the equations do not describe the same situation - do not hedge, do not soften.

**"I found" and "I believe" as authority signals (CORR-04, CORR-08, CORR-10, CORR-13).**
The peer review voice uses first-person epistemic hedges not to soften claims but to locate them precisely: "I found the presentation a great strength"; "I believe it to be suitable"; "As I understand it..." The hedge marks the claim as the reviewer's reading, not the universal truth - which makes the claim more credible because it is bounded. In manuscript prose, use "we find" and "we show" for results that follow from the argument; avoid them for preliminary observations or open questions.

**Personal confessional correspondence mechanics (CORR-01, CORR-02, CORR-03).**
The personal letter register (CORR-01 to CORR-03) shows the voice at maximum amplitude: forensic self-awareness, prosecutorial precision about others' words, extreme concision after long elaboration ("I cannot compartmentalise."), and metaphor used to systematise suffering. These mechanics do not transfer directly to manuscript prose but confirm the personal register observations. The one transferable principle: a one-line conclusion after a long technical build lands harder than any amount of summary prose.

## Manuscript Transfer

Correspondence can teach manuscript prose how to be decisive. It should not import second-person address, tactical irritation, or private relational context into a paper.

The peer review sub-register specifically teaches: open from the largest honest structural claim about the work's contribution; diagnose the root rather than listing symptoms; convert limitations into open questions; use first-person epistemic markers to bound claims precisely; deliver firmness through logical structure, not through tone.
