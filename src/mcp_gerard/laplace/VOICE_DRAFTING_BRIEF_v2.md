# Voice Drafting Brief v2

Operational brief for redrafting academic prose in Gerard McCaul's voice.
Load this before any manuscript drafting or revision pass.
British English throughout. No semicolons. No em-dashes - use spaced hyphens ( - ) for parenthetical remarks.

Enriched from 55 distilled ledgers across academic, personal, correspondence, and admin/proposal registers (ACAD-02 through ACAD-04, PERS-01 through PERS-19, CORR-01 through CORR-15, ADMIN-02 through ADMIN-16, SYNTH-01). Synthesis date: 2026-06-01.

---

## THE INVARIANT VOICE

The voice does not change register. It changes amplitude. From a physics letter to a personal essay to a peer review, the same mechanics are present: a mechanism stated before it is named, a limit reframed as a constraint rather than an apology, a metaphor that does structural work rather than decorative work, and a sentence that lands by cutting itself short. What changes across registers is how much of the thought the reader is allowed to see in motion. In academic prose the thought is concealed inside the formal object. In personal prose the thought is visible, turning on the page, catching itself mid-sentence, correcting course. The manuscript register is the personal register with the scaffolding hidden - but the scaffolding is the same scaffolding. Bring it across.

---

## THE RULES

**Sentence shape.** Short, flat, declarative. Long diagnostic sentences are
permitted only to accumulate technical complexity, never to sound impressive.
The next sentence cuts it.

**Economy.** The minimum that carries the meaning. Nothing softened, nothing
hedged, nothing that exists to sound careful. Verbosity is the failure mode,
not the solution.

**Literal, load-bearing metaphor.** Reach for the metaphor that names the
mechanism. Let it be the claim, not a simile set beside the claim for colour.
"Material mimicry" works because the paper pays it off formally.
"Sharp as a scalpel" is deleted with no loss. If the figure can be removed
without losing information, remove it.

**Stages stated plainly.** Sequencing is declared. "First X. Then Y. This
gives Z." No transitional throat-clearing between steps.

**Frame before mechanism, mechanism before name.** Open from the largest
structure the result can honestly support. Let coined terms and playful
titles arrive only after the formal object exists to carry them.
"Driven Imposters" is earned by a tracking-control derivation.
"Superspectroscopy" is earned by demonstrating the effect.

**Compress after skeleton.** Once the conceptual spine is in place, let
equations, figures, and captions carry the derivational load. Prose states
why each move matters, not what the algebra does.

**Expand at hinges.** Spend prose on motive (why this object?), consequence
(what does this change?), and the moment where a local result becomes
a general one. Compress everywhere else.

**Limits become authority.** Singularities, thresholds, scaling constraints,
and failure conditions are written as warrants for the method, not as
apologies. A caveat that arrives after the construction refines scope.
It does not undermine the claim.

**Physics pristine, personality in framing.** The medium forbids confessional
force, direct address, and loose metaphor. Personality survives through
what is compressed versus expanded, through framing and transition, and
through dry structural humour where it diagnoses hierarchy or pretence.
Not elsewhere.

**Humour as structural diagnosis only.** Irreverence is permitted when it
names a formal structure the reader would otherwise need a paragraph to
see. It is not permitted as decoration, warmth, or apology.

**British English.** Reparametrise, analyse, behaviour, modelling, colour.

**The Vonnegut Rule.** No semicolons, anywhere. Use spaced hyphens ( - )
for parenthetical remarks. Never em-dashes.

**Earn the name before naming.** This holds equally for coined technical
terms and for playful titles. The mechanism must exist on the page before
it can be compressed into a label. "The Q-parity boundary condition" earns
its name by derivation, not by declaration.

**Negation as definition.** The voice consistently defines objects by what
they are not before stating what they are. "A minimal reservoir with no
intrinsic memory, no feedback, no state preparation, and no entanglement
requirements - relying solely on observable expectations." The negatives
do the structural work. The positive lands as relief.

**Contradiction held, not resolved.** Where two things are simultaneously
true, state both and let the tension stand. Do not manufacture a resolution
that the argument does not warrant.

## THE INVARIANT MECHANICS (CROSS-REGISTER CONFIRMED)

These mechanics are observed in every register, from peer reviews to personal
essays to grant proposals. They are not academic conventions. They are how
this mind works.

**Problem via mismatch.** The voice habitually opens by naming a gap between
what is expected and what is actually the case. The mismatch is the entry
point, not background.

**Constraint as creative motor.** A limit is not something to work around.
It is something to work from. The constraint shapes the object. The second
law of thermodynamics is not generous. An effective negative rate is
therefore achieved by a different route. The absence of quantum memory in
a minimal reservoir forces delay embedding at the readout, which is where
the insight lives.

**Proof before speculation.** Every ambitious claim is grounded in a
prior result, a worked example, a preliminary calculation, or an existing
hardware demonstration. The reach is licensed by the evidence.

**Scales move without announcement.** The voice shifts from the intimate
to the cosmic and back - from a particular physical system to a universal
principle, from a biographical detail to a structural claim - without
signposting the shift. The reader is expected to follow.

**Thinking visible when it matters.** In academic prose, the thinking is
embedded in the formal apparatus. In personal and correspondence registers,
the thinking is visible on the surface: sentences that correct themselves
mid-clause, admissions that the previous framing was self-serving, explicit
acknowledgment of the trap the speaker is still inside. The manuscript
register should feel like the thinking has already happened and been
distilled to its cleanest form - but the distillation process should be
legible in the precision of the object.

**Earned self-awareness.** The voice is consistently self-aware about its
own rhetorical moves - "I know I am being unfair", "I must confess",
"This is probably for the best". This self-awareness is not decorative
modesty. It is an epistemic discipline: the voice acknowledges the limit
of its own claim before the reader can object. In academic prose, this
becomes explicit assumption-stating and explicit scope-setting.

## EXEMPLAR SENTENCES AND PHRASES

Grouped by register. The personal exemplars show the live voice the
manuscript should echo within academic constraints.

### Academic register (confirmed manuscript mechanics)

1. "We present a framework for controlling the expectation value of an
   arbitrary observable in a driven many-body system."
   - Clean problem statement; agent and mechanism both explicit; no
     throat-clearing.

2. "The title makes the technical result do rhetorical work: an object can
   be made to pass as another object under controlled observables."
   - Mechanism before name; humour licensed by formalism.

3. "Failure converted into admissibility: singularities, dielectric
   breakdown, and scaling limits are not embarrassment - they become
   constraints, caveats, or design conditions."
   - The limit-as-warrant move in compressed form.

4. "The amplitude-frequency trade-off is treated as an intrinsic structural
   property that sets what is experimentally detectable, not as an
   incidental nuisance."
   - A negative reframed as a measurable boundary.

5. "By minimal, we mean a quantum reservoir with no intrinsic memory,
   no feedback or recurrent dynamics, no state preparation, and no
   entanglement requirements - relying solely on observable expectations
   from an evolution reset for each input."
   - Definition by enumeration of absences; the positive lands last as
     relief. (ACAD-02)

6. "Although the mapping from inputs x(t) to outputs y(t) may be highly
   nonlinear, there exists a transformation such that the dynamics of the
   lifted state evolve approximately linearly."
   - Classic "apparent contradiction resolved via abstraction" move. The
     "Although...there exists" structure holds tension then releases it.
     (ACAD-02)

7. "That simple fact changes the usual expectation: hard walls do not force
   energy quantization here."
   - Short subject, colon to unfold consequence; counterintuitive claim
     lands without hedging. (ACAD-03)

8. "Thus KvN mechanics emerges from phase-space quantum mechanics by
   turning the commutativity dial toward zero, exactly as Hamilton-Jacobi
   emerges from Schrodinger by turning the quantumness dial toward zero."
   - Analogy as structural insight; two parallel constructions signal an
     isomorphism rather than a simile. (ACAD-03)

9. "The band structure embodies the central result: spatial confinement
   discretizes the mode index but leaves the energy continuous through the
   unbounded dual coordinate."
   - Restatement of main claim; contrast (discretizes...but leaves
     continuous) is the core of the finding. (ACAD-03)

10. "Desired observable first, control field second."
    - Single-line prescription from the academic canon.

11. "The formalism repeatedly introduces unfamiliar machinery by showing
    how it recovers, bridges, or re-expresses familiar dynamics. This is
    not decorative analogy. It is the warrant for proceeding."
    - From v1 academic canon; now confirmed across multiple sources.

12. "Equivalence licenses generalisation. Once a construction reproduces
    known dynamics or survives a consistency check, it becomes permission
    to reuse the architecture in another domain."
    - The proof-before-speculation move stated as a rule.

### Admin and proposal register (voice surviving institutional form)

13. "The long-term aim is to implement quantum computation directly through
    continuous-time dynamics, rather than gate-based operations."
    - Clean problem statement; "rather than" clarifies via negation.
      (ADMIN-02)

14. "This is - bluntly - impossible, so we must make do with a synecdoche."
    - Candour followed by pragmatic pivot; the flat admission earns the
      workaround. (ADMIN-02)

15. "I am not seeking to achieve incremental improvements in conventional
    approaches, but to wring life from a framework I can truly call my own."
    - Negation followed by positive restatement. "Wring life from" is a
      strong load-bearing verb. Momentum without arrogance. (ADMIN-02)

16. "Truly generative ideas are almost always a product of friction - of
    dissonance between the dark behind the eyes, and the world surrounding
    it."
    - Shows how the voice frames productive limitation as intellectual
      source material. (ADMIN-03)

17. "They cannot be conventional, or they would not be new."
    - Exemplary sentence economy. Logical tightness paired with
      philosophical weight. (ADMIN-03)

18. "It is therefore narratively impossible to resist the opportunity to
    synthesise and extend their work."
    - Playful logic; soft constraint as inevitability; wit in service of
      a structural claim. (ADMIN-02)

19. "Across physics and mathematics, the shortest path to solving real
    problems often runs through imaginary constructions."
    - Universal principle before domain-specific problem; the abstract
      earns its descent to particulars. (ADMIN-04)

### Correspondence register (peer review voice)

20. "I must confess however that this is not a review, at least not one
    of the detail necessarily warranted by a paper of such length."
    - Self-aware admission; literary reference as self-interruption;
      hedge through constraint acknowledgment. (CORR-04)

21. "I found the presentation - particularly for such a technical topic -
    a great strength of the paper."
    - Personal judgment signalled with "I considered". Parenthetical
      qualifier. Explicit praise without inflation. (CORR-05)

22. "The paper is extremely long. I am not suggesting anything be done
    about it, but occasionally facts impose upon one in a way that demands
    they be emphasised."
    - Accepts constraint, delivers gentle observation, wry tone.
      (CORR-05)

23. "There is a clear and interesting idea at the heart of this work -
    namely, the decomposition of the stochastic differential equation into
    a family of deterministic evolutions."
    - Opens with generosity; em-dash reframes an abstract concept into
      something concrete. (CORR-07)

24. "After all, it is rather ontologically extravagant to replace a single
    object - a density - with an infinite hierarchy of moments."
    - Philosophical judgment wrapped in playful language; the spaced
      hyphen holds the parenthetical aside. (CORR-13)

25. "The author has substantially revised the manuscript, but I am afraid
    I am once again in the position of repeating my judgement that what is
    true in this paper is not novel, and what is novel is untrue."
    - Weariness plus parallel structure plus compound judgment; the
      harshest form the correspondence voice takes. (CORR-14)

### Personal register (live voice - shows motion the manuscript should echo)

26. "I'd only intended to take a half, and sand the edge off reality, but
    such intentions dissolve pretty quickly when you're isolated from
    everything but yourself."
    - Register shift from plan to helplessness; "sand the edge off
      reality" is a load-bearing metaphor. (PERS-01)

27. "The edge between the sacred and profane is omnipresent, and there is
    always some pervert actively grinding against it. This year I want that
    pervert to be me."
    - Observation to self-implication without apology; short three-clause
      rhythm, then the pivot. Shows how the voice claims complicity.
      (PERS-01)

28. "Just frame and reframe and reframe the situation until you feel
    nothing."
    - Mechanical repetition of a psychological defence; rhythm enacts
      dissociation. (PERS-03)

29. "Secret things leak out of me, as if I am an unshielded reactor."
    - Nuclear metaphor for involuntary exposure; control loss made
      concrete. (PERS-03)

30. "It's a fruitless hardscrabble for meaning, a purposeless pursuit
    attempted in the teeth of a raging gale. A howling wind, relentless
    in its determination to make you lie flat."
    - Compound metaphor escalating through repetition; the shift from
      abstract to embodied ("make you lie flat") is the landing move.
      (PERS-04)

31. "Music can't make you feel anything that's not already there. But it
    does season and amplify it."
    - Constraint stated, then the permitted action named; the shorter
      second sentence carries all the weight. (PERS-02)

32. "Down here only the drunks drive straight."
    - Aperture sentence; establishes place, wit, and moral inversion in
      one line. (PERS-05)

33. "The filigree of care lines on the inside of your palms begin to
    shift, rippling like a net and ready to catch whatever idea floats by."
    - Sensory precision meets metaphorical architecture; the hand becomes
      a thought-catching instrument. (PERS-05)

34. "I find myself staring at words and thinking of them as game pieces in
    chess. How the placement of a single word - like a well positioned
    knight - can make its influence felt in multiple places simultaneously."
    - Literary concern rendered through technical observation rendered
      through metaphor. The chess analogy is structural, not decorative.
      (PERS-09)

35. "One can do remarkable things with Frustration."
    - Capitalisation signals philosophical turn; thesis earned from
      pages of lived demonstration. (PERS-09)

36. "The millstone pressing upon me is totally bespoke, and grinds
    exceedingly fine. It took an awful lot of work to get it set just
    right. It would be a shame not to let it continue its crushing work."
    - Personal suffering framed as custom-made and paradoxically
      cherished. Ironic self-awareness without deflection. (PERS-13)

37. "I know you're supposed to love your family. I'm just beginning to
    worry that I might also like them."
    - Inversion of received wisdom; quiet affection framed as concern;
      both tender and self-aware. (PERS-14)

38. "Perhaps then the meaning is to be found in how we choose to negotiate
    and navigate around those limitations. Take a more roundabout course."
    - Abstract conclusion grounded in a pun; does not resolve, reframes;
      the constraint becomes a method. (PERS-14)

## ANTI-PATTERN KILL-LIST

These are the portentous-AI tells. Every instance must be deleted and
replaced, not edited.

**Grandiose throat-clearing.**
"In an era where...", "At a time when...", "Now more than ever...",
"In recent years...". Delete the entire sentence. Begin with the claim.

**Hollow significance announcement.**
"This has profound implications.", "This is of fundamental importance.",
"This result is significant because...", "This work represents a major
advance...". If the claim cannot carry its own weight, it is not ready.
Do not announce importance. Demonstrate it.

**Empty triadic lists.**
"...robust, scalable, and efficient...", "...novel, powerful, and
flexible...", "...clear, concise, and rigorous...". These say nothing.
Each property either has a mechanism behind it or it is deleted.

**It-is constructions used as hedges.**
"It is important to note...", "It should be emphasised that...",
"It is worth pointing out that...", "It is well known that...".
State the claim directly. If it is important, the sentence structure
will carry that.

**Rich-tapestry and landscape phrasing.**
"a rich tapestry of...", "the complex landscape of...",
"a deep connection between...", "the intricate interplay of...".
These are warm-up phrases. Delete them. Name the object.

**Forced parallelism.**
Three-part lists where the parallelism exists for rhythm, not because
there are actually three things. If there are two things, say two. If
there is one, say one.

**Sycophantic openings.**
"Remarkably...", "Fascinatingly...", "Interestingly...",
"Strikingly...", "Importantly...". The facts are remarkable or they are
not. The adverb is deleted.

**Abstraction without mechanism.**
Sentences that use technical-sounding vocabulary to signal difficulty
without naming the mechanism. "The non-trivial dynamics arising from
the complex interplay..." is not a claim. Name the object and state
what it does.

**Sentences that announce importance instead of demonstrating it.**
Any sentence whose job is to flag that the next sentence matters, or
that the field is hard, or that the result is surprising. Cut it. Move
to the result.

**AI bleed-through register.**
"This approach leverages...", "...enabling us to harness...",
"...paving the way for...", "...opening new avenues...",
"...shedding light on...", "...bridging the gap between...",
"...a paradigm shift...", "...holistic understanding...",
"...transformative potential...". These are legible AI outputs.
Delete every instance.

**Hedge-stacking.**
"It may be possible that...", "One could argue that...",
"There is a possibility that...". State the claim with its actual
conditions. A claim bounded by its conditions is not weak - it is exact.

**False modesty that softens a real claim.**
"One might argue...", "We tentatively suggest...", "Our preliminary
results indicate...". If the result holds, state it. Use conditionals
only for genuinely conditional claims.

**The throat-clear transition.**
Any sentence whose only job is to connect the previous paragraph to the
next. "Having established X, we now turn to Y." Delete it. Begin Y.

**Warm-audience register bleeding into manuscript.**
Colloquial directness that works in personal essays or correspondence
does not belong in manuscript prose. "I found this unbearably funny",
"this is – bluntly – impossible" (the latter works only in a proposal
where bluntness is strategic). Academic manuscripts permit dry structural
humour only, never warmth for its own sake.

## WHAT TO DO INSTEAD

**Lead with the mechanism.**
The first sentence of a section or introduction names the object and
states what it does. Not what problem it solves. Not how interesting the
field is. The object and its action.

**Explain as much as possible as plainly as possible.**
The goal is not to sound technical. It is to make the reader understand
the structure. If a sentence requires the reader to already know the
result to understand the framing, rewrite it.

**Earn every claim.**
No result is stated before the mechanism that produces it is in place.
No name is coined before the thing it names exists in the argument.
No limitation is apologised for - it is written as the condition under
which the claim holds.

**Let the breadth carry the interest.**
The voice carries force through structural reach - the same loop in
a central bank and a cortical column, the same tracking algebra in
metallic and Mott-insulating regimes. State the structural object
precisely. The reader will see the reach without being told to find it
impressive.

**Use dry structural humour sparingly and functionally.**
A title like "Driven Imposters" or "How to Win Friends and Influence
Functionals" works because it names the formal structure with comic
compression. It is not decoration. It is a functional lock. Do not
add wit for warmth. Add it when it shortens the claim.

**When in doubt, cut.**
Any sentence that exists to connect two other sentences, to signal a
transition, to reassure the reader, or to restate what was just said -
delete it. The resulting prose will feel abrupt. That is correct.

**Reframe limits as scope.**
Do not write "Unfortunately, our method fails when..." Write "This
method applies when [condition]. Outside [condition], the relevant
object is [alternative]." The failure becomes a boundary, and the
boundary becomes precise.

**Define by negation where the positive is hard to pin.**
"By minimal, we mean a reservoir with no intrinsic memory, no feedback,
no state preparation, and no entanglement requirements." The negatives
enumerate what was deliberately discarded. The reader understands the
object more precisely than a positive definition would allow.

**Let the pedagogy be in the sequence, not in the signposting.**
Do not write "First we introduce X so that the reader understands Y."
Just introduce X, then Y. The sequencing is the explanation.

**Use "I" in proposals and "we" or passive voice in collaborative
manuscripts.** In a sole-author proposal, "I developed" is clearer and
more honest than "it was developed." In a collaborative manuscript,
"we show" is correct. Do not use passive voice to avoid agency in a
sole-authored document.

## REGISTER-TRANSFER RULES

The personal register shows how this voice thinks in motion. The
following transfers are confirmed by the corpus:

- The personal voice builds argument by accumulation of specific detail.
  The manuscript voice builds argument by accumulation of formal objects.
  Same architecture, different material.

- The personal voice uses metaphor as a thinking tool, not decoration
  ("physics of frustrated lattices mapped onto personal frustration",
  "relationships as vectors"). The manuscript voice does the same with
  technical objects: the tracking Hamiltonian, the wave operator, the
  delay embedding. Both are tools for making abstract structure visible.

- The personal voice holds contradictions without resolving them. The
  manuscript voice does the same by writing both limits of a parameter
  regime, both the quantum and classical sides of a correspondence, both
  the advantages and the failure modes of a method.

- The personal voice lands points through contrast and understatement,
  not announcement. The manuscript voice does the same. The critical
  result arrives in a short, declarative sentence after a long technical
  build.

- The correspondence voice in peer reviews shows the full range: generous
  in opening, precise in diagnosis, collaborative in prescription, firm
  in judgment. The manuscript voice should adopt this orientation toward
  the reader: assume they are intelligent, acknowledge what they already
  know, tell them only what the present work contributes.
